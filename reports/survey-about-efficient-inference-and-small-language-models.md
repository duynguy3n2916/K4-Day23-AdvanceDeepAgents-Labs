# Survey on Efficient Inference and Small Language Models

## TL;DR
- Efficient inference techniques like quantization and knowledge distillation have significantly improved the performance of small language models [1].
- Recent studies show that small models can achieve competitive results with larger models through optimized strategies [2].
- Real-world applications, such as Gravity Ads and Qualtrics, demonstrate substantial latency reductions and cost efficiencies [3].

## Background
The surge in applications requiring real-time responses has led to the development of smaller, more efficient language models while maintaining an acceptable level of performance. This survey explores key methodologies, trade-offs, and successful implementations in the field.

## Efficient Inference Techniques
Recent literature highlights various methodologies for enhancing inference efficiency in small language models, including outlier-aware quantization and task-centric acceleration strategies, leading to an improved balance between speed and model quality [1].

## Size and Performance Trade-offs
The exploration of trade-offs between model size and performance indicates that through parameter sharing and optimization, small language models can achieve near-comparable effectiveness to larger models under specific contexts. However, effectiveness varies based on architecture [2].

## Real-world Applications
Successful case studies demonstrate significant improvements in latency and operational costs for small language models in action, with notable examples of companies achieving remarkable speed-ups and cost reductions [3].

## Trends and Open Problems
Current trends emphasize accelerating small language models while addressing the balance of trade-offs in various applications. Open questions revolve around optimizing architectures without losing essential performance capabilities.
